# Shape Up glossary

These definitions are paraphrased from the *Shape Up* glossary
(https://basecamp.com/shapeup/4.5-appendix-06), with a few practical notes
added.

- **Appetite:** the time we *want* to spend on a project, set before designing.
  It's the opposite of an estimate. Small batch is 1–2 weeks, and big batch is
  6 weeks.
- **Baseline:** what customers do today without the thing being built. Compare
  your work *down* to it, not up to an ideal.
- **Bet:** a commitment of a team to a project for one cycle, uninterrupted,
  with the expectation that it finishes. A bet has a payout, is a commitment,
  and caps the downside.
- **Betting table:** a short meeting during cool-down where the people with
  final authority choose which pitches to bet on next cycle.
- **Big batch:** one project that occupies a team for a whole cycle and ships
  at the end.
- **Breadboard:** a UI concept made of places, affordances, and connection
  lines, all in words, with no visual styling.
- **Chowder:** a list for loose tasks that fit no scope. It's suspicious if it
  holds more than about 3–5 items.
- **Circuit breaker:** by default, projects that don't ship within their cycle
  are cancelled rather than extended.
- **Cleanup mode:** the last phase before a new product launches. There's no
  shaping and no team boundaries, work ships continuously in small bites, and
  final-cut decisions are made. It lasts at most two cycles.
- **Cool-down:** a two-week gap between cycles for bug fixes, exploration, and
  the betting table.
- **Cycle:** a six-week period of uninterrupted work on shaped projects.
- **De-risk:** improving the odds of shipping within one cycle by removing
  rabbit holes during shaping.
- **Discovered tasks:** tasks found by doing the real work. They make up the
  bulk of a project.
- **Downhill:** the phase where every unknown is solved and only execution
  remains.
- **Elements:** the concrete, narrow parts of a shaped solution. They're what
  shaping produces.
- **Fat-marker sketch:** a very low-fidelity sketch drawn so thick that detail
  is impossible.
- **Grab-bag:** a pseudo-project with no single driving problem, such as a
  "redesign", a "2.0", or an unfocused refactor. It's also a badly named scope,
  like "front-end" or "bugs."
- **Hill chart:** a diagram showing each scope's position on a spectrum from
  unknown, to known, to done.
- **Iceberg:** a scope where the back end is far more complex than the UI. An
  *upside-down iceberg* is the reverse.
- **Imagined tasks:** tasks assumed just from thinking about the project, as
  opposed to discovered tasks.
- **Layer cake:** a scope with thin, evenly spread back-end work under the UI,
  sized by its UI surface.
- **Level of abstraction:** how much detail is left in or out when describing
  a problem or solution.
- **Must-haves:** tasks that must be finished before a scope counts as done.
- **Nice-to-haves:** tasks marked with a leading `~`, done only if time allows
  and usually cut.
- **No-gos:** things explicitly excluded from the concept to fit the appetite.
  They're one of the five pitch ingredients.
- **Out of bounds:** use cases explicitly not supported by the project. They
  feed into the no-gos.
- **Pitch:** a write-up of a shaped project for the betting table, covering
  problem, appetite, solution, rabbit holes, and no-gos.
- **Production mode:** the phase of a new product after its architecture has
  settled, run as standard Shape Up. "Shipping" means merging to main.
- **Rabbit hole:** a part of a project too unknown, complex, or open-ended to
  bet on as is. It gets patched, cut, or declared out of bounds during shaping.
- **R&D mode:** the earliest phase of a new product. A senior team spikes the
  core to set the load-bearing architecture, and nobody expects it to ship.
- **Raw idea:** a request or idea expressed in words and not yet shaped. The
  default response is "Interesting. Maybe some day."
- **Scope:** a part of a project that can be built, integrated, and finished
  independently of the rest, typically in a few days.
- **Scope hammering:** forcefully questioning a design, implementation, or use
  case to cut scope so the work fits the time box.
- **Shape:** to make an abstract idea concrete by defining the key elements of
  a solution before betting on it. Shaped work is rough, solved, and bounded.
- **Six weeks:** the cycle length. It's long enough to finish something
  meaningful and short enough to feel the deadline from the start.
- **Small batch:** a set of 1–2-week projects that one team ships within a
  single cycle.
- **Time horizon:** the longest period over which a deadline is still felt from
  the beginning, which is six weeks.
- **Uphill:** the phase where there are still unknowns or unsolved problems.
